# `world_class@2` over the XG corpus

**`world_class@2` agrees with XG as well as the 2026-09-20 `world_class` did,
and it took 1.83x less time to get there.** The mean gap to XG's match PR went
from 0.4228 to 0.4196, which is not a significant change. Cube agreement rose by
1.2 points, and checker agreement fell by 0.6 points, all of it on near-ties.

Run 2026-10-21 on the Studio (24-core M2 Ultra) at `607a768`, over the same 493
matches (84,008 plies, 986 player-match ratings) as the
[preset study](../docs/PRESET_ACCURACY.md) and the
[September re-run](2026-09-20-corpus-rerun.md). It took 3 h 31 m (12,641 s),
against 6 h 25 m for the September `world_class` on the same machine. The run
was never paused.

## What changed since the September run

`world_class@2` is the September preset plus three changes, all on the
`feat/preset-versions` branch:

1. **Staged checker screening.** Moves go 1-ply, then 2-ply, then N-ply, and a
   middle tier deepens only the screen's close moves.
2. **Cube routing.** A cube is deepened to 4-ply within 0.12 of its double or
   take point (was 0.08), and any wrong cube action is rolled out (was: only
   one wrong by more than 0.02).
3. **Seeded rollouts.** A rollout tier rolls out the best, a close runner-up and
   the played move, chosen by the tier below. bgsage's own selection rolled out
   3.85 moves a decision.

## Method

Same as September. Each output file is a copy of the corpus document with the
`world_class@2` block appended, so it is paired by construction against the XG
block in the same file. It is also paired against the September `world_class`
block, read from `gva-rerun/wc/`. Match PR per block comes from
`gvformat.stats.compute_aggregates` over a single-block projection.

**The measurement reproduces before it is trusted.** Re-deriving the September
block gives a mean gap of **0.4228**, exactly the published figure, on
n = 61,460 scored checker plays and 8,334 scored cubes, the published counts.

## Result

| | mean gap to XG | bias (XG − ours) | PR within 1 of XG | p90 gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `world_class@2` | **0.4196** | −0.110 | 92.0% | 0.915 |
| `world_class` (2026-09-20) | 0.4228 | −0.120 | 91.7% | 0.936 |

Paired, on |gap to XG| per rating: **−0.0031**, 95% CI [−0.0101, +0.0039],
t = −0.88. 448 ratings are closer and 416 further. The two blocks differ from
each other by 0.060 PR on average. 10 of 986 ratings moved by more than 0.5,
and the two largest (1.90 and 1.84) both moved toward XG.

### Checker play

Same best move as XG, by highest-equity alternative with hop chains collapsed,
over the 61,460 decisions XG scores:

| XG's best-vs-2nd gap | n | September | `@2` |
|---|---|---|---|
| under 0.005 | 11,491 | 53.24% | 51.70% |
| 0.005 – 0.02 | 12,900 | 80.35% | 79.22% |
| 0.02 – 0.05 | 11,656 | 91.36% | 91.01% |
| 0.05 and up | 25,413 | 94.73% | 94.72% |
| all | 61,460 | 83.31% | 82.72% |

The whole 0.6-point drop is on plays XG separates by less than 0.02, where
either choice costs almost nothing. These are the like-for-like figures. The
published 90.3% is still not reproduced, as the September write-up records.

### Cube

Same optimal action as XG (no double, double/take, double/pass or too good),
taken from each block's own equities, over the 8,334 cubes XG scores:

| | n | September | `@2` |
|---|---|---|---|
| no-double positions | 4,937 | 95.24% | 97.51% |
| doubles | 1,651 | 95.15% | 94.91% |
| takes | 1,041 | 92.41% | 92.22% |
| passes | 705 | 95.89% | 95.46% |
| all | 8,334 | 94.92% | 96.16% |

By the level `@2` judged the cube at, it agrees with XG on 98.0% at 3-ply,
99.3% at 4-ply and 88.7% at `truncated2`. The pattern is the one
`PRESET_ACCURACY.md` describes: a rolled-out cube agrees with XG less often.
That is a different estimator from XG's 4-ply, not necessarily a worse one, so
the small drop on actual doubles, takes and passes, which `@2` now always rolls
out when wrong, is expected rather than a defect.

## Cost on three machines

The six-match cost benchmark from
[the September cross-machine study](2026-09-19-cross-machine-costs.md), re-run
with the same matches, the same `usercost.py` and auto `--jobs`, in ms per ply:

| preset | M2 Ultra | M3 (8c) | i5-7600 (4c) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `very_quick` | 5.8 → 6.9 | 11.6 → (17.5) | 40.8 → 40.9 |
| `fast` | 9.6 → 9.4 | 31.4 → 27.0 | 75.5 → 62.6 |
| `deep` | 18.0 → 13.2 | 65.5 → 44.1 | 143.0 → 97.0 |
| `world_class` | 291.3 → 154.3 | 1371 → 612 | 2048 → 1025 |

`very_quick` is the control, since its code did not change. It holds on the
i5. On the M3 the run started while the machine was busy indexing in the
background (load average 10 at launch), so that row is not comparable. The
rows after it ran later; any leftover contamination could only overstate them.
`world_class` is 1.9x, 2.2x and 2.0x faster. The September
`world_class_fast` measured 166 / 611 / 1016, so `world_class@2` now costs
what it did, to within 7%, on every machine.

## What this licenses

- Retiring `world_class_fast`. It existed to be the cheaper strong preset, and
  `world_class@2` now costs what it used to, on all three machines. Paired on 11 matches,
  `world_class_fast@2` took 74% of `world_class@2`'s time for PR 0.13 away from
  it.
- Stating that the `@2` changes cost nothing in XG agreement. It is **not** a
  claim that they improved accuracy: the change is inside the noise.
